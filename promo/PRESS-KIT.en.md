## 2. Store /itch page -- long description

> The lawyer's letter arrived first. The Eighteenth Post -- your mother's inn -- has stood empty for six years. Open it within thirty days, or the land is taken by law.
>
> So you ride.
>
> The road is not a line out and back. It is a figure-eight that crosses over itself, and you only find that out if you keep riding long enough to come back past where you started. Sixteen stations sit along it. Five of them hold a piece of something your mother used to do. Each one wants three visits to give it up.
>
> A man from Yunling Cultural Tourism wants the thing under the hot spring. He tells you so three times: first politely, then insistently, then without pretending. His offer is reasonable. His reason is not.
>
> And the mountain keeps its own account.
>
> The keeper of the road remembers the road, not himself. You will remember the way home for everyone you pass. The price is that no one remembers you.
>
> The game never tells you this directly. You get it from four worn stone markers, three phone calls, and the fact that every station knows your name but no one knows theirs.
>
> -- Runs in any modern browser. 12.8 MB total. Built to hold 60 fps on hardware that cannot normally run 3D. --

## 4. Elevator pitch -- 45 seconds

> Most narrative games stop the world to tell you a story. This one refuses to.
>
> You ride a single 1.2 km figure-eight loop through mist-layered mountains, with your mother's inn on a thirty-day legal deadline behind you. Every story beat arrives in the dialogue strip while the bicycle is already moving, because stopping the player is the one thing this game will not do -- people who stop do not remember the road.
>
> Sixteen stations. Five of them hold a fragment of something your mother used to do, and each wants three visits to give it up. You collect cloudshadow, tea steam, a remembered zither phrase, bamboo in the rain, and a single bird. At the end they are pressed into a postcard you can write on or leave blank.
>
> The whole thing ships in 12.8 MB, with no textures at all, because the buildings are generated in code and it still runs on a phone.

## 5. Press release -- short form (~150 words)

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**Gift No.188: a quiet cycling narrative about the road your mother disappeared on**
*Indie developer Liang Zhuowen releases a 20-minute 3D cycling game that ships in 12.8 MB*

[City, Date] -- Independent developer Liang Zhuowen today released *Gift No.188*, a 3D cycling narrative game played entirely on a single 1,228.8-metre figure-eight road.

Players ride Miles Lu up the mountain to re-walk the route his mother disappeared on, under a thirty-day legal deadline to reopen her inn. Along the way, sixteen roadside stations offer five small joys -- cloudshadow, tea, a zither phrase, bamboo in the rain, and a single bird -- each requiring three visits before it gives anything up.

The game has no combat and no fail state. It runs in any modern browser, ships in 12.8 MB, and was engineered to hold frame rate on low-end hardware by generating its architecture in code and using no textures at all.

*Gift No.188* is available in English and Simplified Chinese.

*Miles Lu, the Eighteenth Post, Route No.188 and Zheng Duo are fictional.*

## 6. Developer bio -- short

**Liang Zhuowen (梁卓文)** is an independent game developer. *Gift No.188* began as a Godot 4.6 project and was ported to the web in three.js and TypeScript, with gameplay, numbers and script preserved exactly. The work is characterised by small, verifiable claims: a headless regression suite with hard geometric invariants, procedural architecture instead of an asset pipeline, and a rendering budget designed around what weak hardware is actually short of.
