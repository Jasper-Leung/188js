## 2. Store page — long description

> He had already left the job he was supposed to stay in. The family business was his to take or not take, and he had not said yes. The Eighteenth Post -- the inn his family has kept for generations -- had stood empty for six years.
>
> Then a lawyer's letter gave him thirty days to open it. After that the land goes to someone else, and something under the hot spring goes with it.
>
> So he rides.
>
> The road is a figure-eight that crosses over itself. You only find that out if you keep riding long enough to come back past where you started.
>
> **Touch an old object and it remembers.** A fragment plays back -- someone else's memory, from when they were the one who still had plans. Six seconds. A room, a season, a face going out of focus.
>
> Three rules, and the third one is the whole game: you get a fragment, not a life. You get the person, never the reason. **And you cannot change the past.** Not one thing you see can be altered, warned about, or fixed. You are always arriving late.
>
> Eight hundred years ago, a famous poet came up this road, gave up the life that had been arranged for him, and stayed. He did not die here disappointed. He died here happy -- and left his things behind, one in every station, like a trail for whoever came next.
>
> Sixteen stations. Five of them hold a small joy -- cloudshadow, tea, a remembered zither phrase, bamboo in the rain, and one bird. Each wants three visits before it gives anything up. At the end they are pressed into a postcard you can write on, or leave blank.
>
> No combat. No fail state. No timer you can lose.
>
> 12.8 MB total. No textures -- the buildings are generated in code and the surfaces are computed in shaders. It still runs on hardware that cannot open a normal 3D game.

## 5. Press release — short form

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**Gift No.188: touch an old object, and it remembers**
*Developer Liang Zhuowen adds a memory mechanic and an eight-hundred-year mystery to a 3D cycling game that ships in 12.8 MB*

[City, Date] -- Independent developer Liang Zhuowen today released an updated build of *Gift No.188*, a 3D cycling narrative game, adding a story built around a single mechanic: touching an old object plays back a fragment of its owner's memory.

In the game, Miles Lu has already quit his job and is weighing whether to take over his family's inn when a lawyer's letter gives him thirty days to reopen it or lose the land. Riding the 1,228.8-metre figure-eight loop, he begins finding objects -- an ink brush, a chipped celadon cup, a folded letter -- that each play back six seconds of someone else's memory. He cannot change anything he sees.

Following the trail, he learns that a famous poet came up the same road eight hundred years earlier, gave up the life arranged for him, stayed, and died there happy.

*Gift No.188* has no combat and no fail state, runs in any modern browser, and ships in 12.8 MB with no textures -- every building on the route is generated in code.

*Miles Lu, the Eighteenth Post, Route No.188 and the eight-hundred-year poet are fictional.*

## 6. Developer bio

**Liang Zhuowen (梁卓文)** is an independent game developer. *Gift No.188* began as a Godot 4.6 project and was ported to the web in three.js and TypeScript, with gameplay, numbers and script preserved exactly. The work is characterised by small verifiable claims: a headless regression suite with hard geometric invariants, procedural architecture instead of an asset pipeline, and a rendering budget designed around what weak hardware is actually short of.
